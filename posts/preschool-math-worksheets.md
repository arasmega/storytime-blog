*Updated September 2026 with our most downloaded worksheets.*

Your child can tell you exactly how many nuggets their sibling got versus how many they got, down to the last one. But ask them to point to the number 4 on a page and suddenly it's a mystery. That gap is completely normal, and it's exactly what preschool maths worksheets are for.

Early maths isn't about speed or getting sums right. It's about building the link between *this many things* and *this squiggle on the page*. Counting out loud, tracing numbers, matching fingers to dots, feeding a hungry cartoon character the right number of watermelon slices.

Below are six of our most downloaded maths printables for ages 3-6. Print one, grab crayons, and keep it short and cheerful.

## **Making preschool maths worksheets actually work at home**

A worksheet is a prompt for a conversation, not a test. How you sit beside your child matters more than how many boxes get filled in.

- **Keep it to 10 minutes.** One page, then crayons away. Ending on a high means they'll say yes next time.
- **Count out loud together the first time.** Then let them try the second row alone.
- **Let the fingers do their job.** Finger counting isn't cheating, it's a child's first calculator.
- **Don't correct straight away.** Say "let's count again together" and let them find it themselves.
- **Take it off the page.** Count kuih on the plate, shoes at the door, cousins at a family gathering. School holidays are perfect for this.

If your child loses interest halfway through, that's information, not failure. Fold the page, save it for the weekend, and count the stairs on the way up to bed instead.
