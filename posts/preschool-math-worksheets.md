Your child can count to twenty at full volume in the car. But ask them how many biscuits are on the plate, and suddenly it's "um... five?" when there are clearly three. That gap between reciting numbers and actually understanding them is completely normal, and it's exactly what good preschool math worksheets are for.

Early numeracy isn't really about drilling sums. It's about helping a child connect the word "four", the squiggle 4, and four actual things they can poke with a finger. Once those three click together, everything else gets easier.

The six worksheets below are all one page each and free to download. Each one is designed so a 3 to 6 year old can finish before the wriggling starts. Print one, grab the crayons, and you're set.

## **Making preschool math worksheets actually work at home**

Worksheets are a tool, not a test. A few small habits make a huge difference to how much your child gets out of them:

- **One page at a time.** A finished single sheet beats three abandoned ones. Stop while they still want more.
- **Say the numbers out loud.** Counting aloud is where the learning happens; the pencil work just records it.
- **Let them use their fingers.** Finger counting isn't cheating, it's how young brains hold numbers steady.
- **Colouring counts.** The colouring sections build pencil grip and give your child a break between counting tasks.
- **Repeat the same sheet.** Print two copies. Doing a worksheet again a week later is when it moves from "figured it out" to "just knows it".
